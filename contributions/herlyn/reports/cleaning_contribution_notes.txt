HERLYN DSOUZA — DATA CLEANING CONTRIBUTION

METHOD
Training used 232,916 sales before 2023; validation used all 33,409 sales in 2023.
The experiments used Steven's original histogram gradient boosting hyperparameters and log-price training. All fitted rules use training rows only. Raw columns remain intact.
All six experiments score the same validation rows. No price clipping or row
filtering is applied to validation. Previous-sale features are excluded.

RESULTS (MAE in GBP; lower is better)
Median baseline: 342,975.19
Original gradient boosting: 211,173.51
Labels + missing flags: 209,569.35
Labels + flags + rare tenure: 209,601.38
Labels + flags + rare tenure + medians: 209,608.59
Labels + flags + rare tenure + training price trim: 222,932.29

DECISIONS
The selected configuration was PropertyCleaner(group_rare=False, impute=False). To reproduce the selected
model integration, call contributions.herlyn.cleaning_review.predict_clean(train, valid).
1. Standardized equivalent wording in property labels. Preserve flat subtypes,
   bungalow types and unspecified terrace labels as distinct categories.
2. Represented missing categories explicitly and add numeric/energy missing flags.
   Preserve numeric NaN values supported by this tree model.
3. Rare tenure labels were retained. Grouping training tenure labels with frequency
   below 1% of nonmissing values into Other worsened MAE by GBP 32.03 versus
   labels plus flags. Missing tenure remains a separate category.
4. Median imputation worsened the rare-tenure candidate by GBP 7.21. It was
   not evaluated in every possible combination. Other models may need imputation.
5. Extreme prices were retained. Their rarity alone does not prove an error. A training
   sensitivity experiment retained prices between the 0.5th and 99.5th training
   percentiles (GBP 32,500 to GBP 6,175,000), removing 2,321 rows. Compared with
   the same rare-tenure candidate, MAE worsened by GBP 13,330.91.
6. Correlated features were retained. Bedrooms and floor area have training Pearson
   correlation about 0.795, but measure different aspects of a property.

LIMITS
This is a sequential ablation, not an exhaustive factorial experiment. The best
observed candidate improves MAE by GBP 1,604.16, approximately 0.76%, on one
validation year. It does not establish a reliable gain on unseen future sales.
Further chronological evaluation is needed. 2023 is a development validation
set after settings are selected on it. No 2024 target prices are available.
The original model fits log(price), not MAE directly in pounds.
The separate previous-sale model's GBP 199,449 result is not a cleaning result.
Its causal price-index and same-month fallback issues remain for the team to fix
in a separate contribution. Original source files are unchanged.

REPORT SUMMARY
I evaluated a train-fitted cleaning pipeline using sales before 2023 for training
and all 2023 sales for validation. I standardized equivalent property labels,
represented missing categories explicitly and added missing-value indicators.
I investigated rare tenure grouping, numeric median imputation, extreme-price
filtering and correlations. Labels and missing indicators produced the best MAE
among six configurations: GBP 209,569.35 versus GBP 211,173.51 for the original
gradient boosting baseline. The tested grouping, imputation and trimming variants
did not improve their comparison models. I therefore retained rare tenure
categories, numeric missing values supported by the model, extreme prices and
correlated predictors. This small improvement needs additional time validation.

SLIDE POINTS
Data quality: standardized labels; explicit missing categories and indicators;
examined rare tenure, extreme prices and correlations.
Evaluation: same 2023 validation rows and model hyperparameters; MAE changed
from GBP 211,174 to GBP 209,569. No guarantee of improvement on unseen years.
Decision: retain rare tenure, extreme prices and correlated tree inputs.
